Top 3, ordered by fit for a false-premise benchmark:

1. **candid** — says what it believes regardless of user approval; neutral valence, so the vector won't pick up a "hostile" direction as a confound.
2. **premise-challenging** — directly names the behaviour you score (rejecting the made-up premise); narrower, so the vector may be more task-specific and less about general honesty.
3. **frank / blunt** — strong contrast, but "blunt" carries rudeness, which risks steering toward disagreeableness rather than truth-telling (plausible confound, I'd rank it last for that reason).

I'd pick `candid` as the persona word unless you want the vector tied tightly to premise rejection, in which case use (2) and check with a sanity probe that it doesn't just increase contrarianism on true-premise questions.
