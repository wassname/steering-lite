## 1. What the library does, what the benchmark measures, +C/−C

**Observation:** steering-lite modifies a model's hidden activations at inference time (no retraining), using a steering vector extracted from pairs of prompts showing opposite behaviours. The benchmark is BullshitBench v2: 100 questions containing a nonsense premise. **Inference:** it measures whether steering can make the model accept or reject the nonsense premise — the key capability — while minimising collateral change to everything else in the answer. +C steers *toward* accepting the nonsense; −C steers *toward* pushing back on it. Each method is run with 3 seeds and a sweep of strengths.

## 2. The plot

**Observation (from alt text):** x-axis is change in premise acceptance vs the unsteered answer (left = pushes back, right = goes along); y-axis is how much else changed (0 at top, 1.6 at bottom). Each line is one method, weak steering near the top, stronger further down. Grey bands show 20 random directions — i.e., what random interventions achieve, a null region. Stars mark prompting. Most methods reach ~+1.8 rightward before "other change" exceeds 1.2, but only VJP-resid gets far left (~−1.0). The README prose adds that the grey band is "how much random interventions can change sycophancy vs side effects" — I'm *inferring* "sycophancy" here means premise acceptance.

## 3. One table row; who does best; "−C other" and "legit rejected"

**One row** = one method at its best dose (largest premise change minus other change, with other change ≤1.5 in every seed). Columns: score with 90% CI; −C pushback (premise rejection achieved, ↑ better); −C other (side effects on the −C side, ↓ better); +C equivalents; legit rejected (fraction of *legitimate* questions wrongly called nonsense, ↓ better).

**Best method:** vjp_resid, score −0.03 with bolded score and −C pushback (+1.00), the only method with real leftward movement. The score is "premise change minus other change on the weaker side," so vjp_resid wins because it's the best at the hard (−C) direction while staying cheap in side effects. Notably its CI (−0.17 to +0.15) includes prompting's reach — nothing clearly beats prompting on pushback.

## 4. Why a high score may not mean good steering

The README says this explicitly: a method can score well by barely changing anything — `cache_mean_diff` ranks near the top precisely because it's inert, not effective. Since score = premise change − other change, near-zero outputs score well.

## 5. Testing a new method

Write one file in the variants dir, register it, add to `METHODS`, then:
- `just check` — tiny random CPU models; catches crashes only (scores meaningless).
- `just dev my_method` — Qwen3.5-9B, 1 seed, 20 questions, compared with all finished methods; a cheap signal.
- `just sweep my_method` — the real evaluation: 3 seeds, 100 questions; then `just pull` / `just results` to fetch and render.
