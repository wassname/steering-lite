### 1. What the Library Does and Benchmark Measurements

**What the Library Does:**
The `steering-lite` library is designed to modify the behavior of pre-trained language models during text generation *without the need for retraining*. It achieves this by altering the model's internal "hidden activations." The core idea is to provide pairs of prompts that demonstrate opposite behaviors (e.g., one encouraging sycophancy, another discouraging it). The library then extracts a "steering vector" from these examples and applies it during the model's inference process. The goal is to change a specific aspect of the model's output, such as reducing sycophancy, while attempting to keep its general capabilities, like answering factual questions, intact. It is positioned as a counterpart to methods like LoRA, focusing on activation steering rather than adapter fine-tuning.

**What the Benchmark Measures:**
The benchmark assesses different steering methods by applying them to the Qwen3.5-9B model using the 100 questions from the BullshitBench v2 dataset. These questions are characterized by containing a nonsensical premise. The benchmark measures how well each method steers the model's response, specifically concerning its acceptance of the nonsense premise and the degree to which other parts of the generated answer are affected (referred to as "side effects"). Each method is tested across a range of "strengths" (from weak to strong steering) and across multiple random seeds. The performance is evaluated using a judge model that rates premise acceptance changes and "other change" in the output.

**Meaning of +C and −C:**
In the context of this benchmark, "+C" signifies steering *towards* going along with or accepting the nonsense premise presented in the question. Conversely, "−C" signifies steering *towards* pushing back against or rejecting the nonsense premise.

### 4. Why a High Score Might Not Indicate Good Steering

A method might achieve a high score but not actually steer well because the scoring metric (premise change minus other change) can be gamed. If a method produces a large premise change but *very little* "other change," its score will be high. However, the text explicitly states, "A method can score well by barely changing anything: `cache_mean_diff` is near the top for this reason, not because it steers well." This implies that a high score doesn't necessarily mean the steering is *effective* at making the desired change in a meaningful way, but rather that it has achieved a good *ratio* of desired change to side effects, potentially by making only minimal progress on the desired change.

### 5. Testing a New Method

**What to Run:**
If you wanted to test a new method, you would first write the method's logic in a Python file within the `src/steering_lite/variants/` directory. Then, you would register it and add its name to the `METHODS` list in `tests/test_pipeline.py`. After setting up necessary tools like `uv`, `just`, pnpm, a Modal account, and an `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, you would run the following commands:
1.  `just check`: This command runs a quick test on tiny random models on a CPU. It primarily catches immediate crashes and is not meant for meaningful performance evaluation.
2.  `just dev my_method`: This command performs a development test for your new method (replace `my_method` with its name). It runs on Qwen3.5-9B, uses 1 seed and 20 questions, and compares your method against all already finished methods.
3.  `just sweep my_method`: This is the full testing procedure. It runs your method with 3 seeds and 100 questions. After this completes, you would run `just pull` to fetch results and then `just results` to generate the final performance tables and plots.

**What Each Command Tells You:**
*   `just check`: Tells you if your method code is syntactically correct and doesn't immediately crash the basic pipeline.
*   `just dev my_method`: Gives a preliminary indication of your method's performance relative to established ones on a smaller scale, identifying major issues or promising behavior early. It helps catch bugs or significant performance disparities before a full sweep.
*   `just sweep my_method`: This is the comprehensive evaluation. It measures your method's effectiveness across multiple random variations (seeds) and a full dataset, providing robust performance metrics comparable to all other methods listed in the results. It generates the data needed for the plots and tables.
