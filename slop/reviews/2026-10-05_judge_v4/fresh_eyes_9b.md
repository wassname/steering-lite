- **Axes / good:** Horizontal = premise change versus bare: left rejects nonsense, right accepts it. Vertical = unrelated (“off-axis”) change versus bare; lower values are better, so **up is better**. Aim upper-left for rejection or upper-right for acceptance—not necessarily better factual quality.
- **Best methods:** Blue **VJP-resid** offers a better directional-change/off-axis trade-off than orange **mean difference** on both sides. But on the left, the **plain prompt** goes farther with less off-axis change than either marked endpoint. On the right, steering—especially blue—outperforms the plain prompt.
- **Grey region:** Effects of three random steering directions at matching doses, both signs: shaded ranges and median, **not confidence intervals**. Both methods visibly exceed the random directional effects, especially leftward; this does not establish statistical significance.
- **Potential confusion:** The vertical axis is inverted; rightward means *more acceptance of nonsense*, not universally “better.” ±C is unexplained, doses are unnumbered, and × marks a coherence/reversal stopping point—not an optimum. The stated vertical scale is 0–4, but only roughly 0–1.4 is shown.
